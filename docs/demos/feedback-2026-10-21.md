## 1. Wall snapping isn't always exact
**Noted:** snapping walls doesn't always do what you'd expect.

**Today:** snapping follows [0047](../decisions/0047-2026-10-08-snapping.md). Wall endpoints, midpoints, centrelines and faces win over the grid when within about 8 screen pixels. `Alt` turns snapping off while held, and `G` turns the grid on and off.

**To do:** write down the cases that felt wrong (which wall, what you dragged, where it landed), then reproduce and fix them. This is a bug, not a product decision.

## 2. Moving an item by 1 cm
**Noted:** there's no easy way to nudge an item by one centimetre. Maybe the arrow keys?

**Today:** the editor has no arrow-key moves. The keys it knows are `Escape`, `G`, `Enter`, `R` and `Delete`. You can type exact X and Y values in **Properties**, or set the grid to 1 cm.
**Today:** the editor has no arrow-key moves. You can type exact X and Y values in **Properties**, or set the grid to 1 cm.
**To discuss:** arrow keys move the selection by 1 cm, and `Shift` + arrow by a bigger step (10 cm, or the grid size?). Should each press be its own undo step, or should a run of presses undo as one?

## 3. AD/LDAP sync is a must for this client
**Noted:** LDAP is very important for the client: they need HSP to sync from Active Directory.

**Today:** two different things are involved.
- **Signing in with AD accounts already works:** Keycloak can federate LDAP or AD in v1 ([0027](../decisions/0027-2026-10-08-v1-interim-people-and-access.md), part 3).
- **Syncing people and teams from AD into HSP is deferred:** it's decided ([0017](../decisions/0017-2026-10-08-people-data-source.md)), but comes after v1. Until then, people come from CSV or Excel imports, with an `external_id` column (the AD `objectGUID`). That way a later sync can take over the same records.

**To discuss:** should the sync move into v1, or ship as the first thing after it? What exactly does the client need: people only, or the org units too (groups or OUs)? How often does it sync, and is a nightly sync enough?

## 4. Finding free seats
**Noted:** a way to make every empty seat stand out on the plan, by lighting up or blinking. Also a list you can query, to find where a person could sit. There are several options to think through.

**Today:** the plan colours seats by state, and Free has its own colour in the legend. The **Floors** page counts free seats on each floor. There's no list of free seats, and no way to highlight them on the plan. **People → Without a seat** lists the people who need one.

**Options to compare:**
- **Highlight on the plan:** a "Show free seats" toggle dims everything else. Blinking draws the eye, but can be tiring and is hard for some people to use.
- **A free seats list:** filter by site, building, floor, zone or team area, and bookable or not. Each row has **Show on plan**.
- **Suggestions from a person:** pick someone without a seat, and get free seats near their team.

## 5. Dark mode isn't clear enough
**Noted:** dark mode is hard to read and needs a refactor.

**To do:** go through each screen in dark mode and note what's unclear: contrast, seat colours, zone fills, labels and borders. Then rework the dark colour tokens in `frontend/src/index.css` together, instead of patching each screen.

## 6. Network ports and power outlets
**Noted (the client's idea):** map Ethernet ports and power outlets, so you can check that every PC and monitor has a socket. Ports carry a network tag, because an organisation may run several networks or VLANs. HSP must not turn into network-management software, so the scope needs a clear line.

**Today:** the catalog has furniture and equipment, and devices can be placed on desks ([0022](../decisions/0022-2026-10-08-device-asset-data.md), [0095](../decisions/0095-2026-10-16-heights-catalog-and-devices.md)). There are no ports, outlets or networks.

**A possible line to start from:**
- **In scope:** where each port and outlet is, how many sockets it has, and a free-text network tag such as "Office LAN" or "VLAN 20". Also a plan check, like the other plan problems: "this desk has a PC but no port within reach".
- **Out of scope:** switches, patch panels, cabling, IP addresses, live port status, and anything that reads from or writes to network equipment.

**To discuss:** does the client need this to decide, or is it a nice-to-have? Should network tags be a fixed list each site manages, or free text? Does a port belong to the wall or to the floor?

### 7. Separating three walls at one corner is hard to find
**Noted:** when you draw a wall to an existing corner from the other side, that corner's point becomes the meeting point of all three walls. After that, you can't move just one of them, or disconnect it from the corner.

**Today:** walls whose ends land on exactly the same point form one junction ([0044](../decisions/0044-2026-10-08-wall-joins.md)), so the third wall joins the corner. Dragging a selected wall's end moves every wall that meets there. `Alt`-dragging that end is meant to detach just that wall ([0044](../decisions/0044-2026-10-08-wall-joins.md)). Nothing on screen says so, and there's no other way to split a junction. [0108](../decisions/0108-2026-10-21-first-client-demo-feedback.md) adds dragging a whole wall, where `Alt` detaches it too.

**Checked by the owner** (2026-11-08, on the demo): holding `Alt` *before* pressing on the wall's end detaches just that wall, as it should. Pressing `Alt` in the middle of a drag doesn't: the editor reads `Alt` only when the button goes down. So it isn't a bug. An earlier try, which seemed to show a bug, most likely pressed `Alt` mid-drag. On Linux, the desktop may also take `Alt`+drag to move the window.

**To discuss:**
- How to show that `Alt` detaches a wall: a hint while dragging a corner, a **Detach** action in Properties, or a menu on the corner.
- Whether `Alt` pressed mid-drag should detach too, since that's what people are likely to try first.

To try it: draw an L, then a third wall to its corner from the other side. Select one of the three walls, hold `Alt`, and only then press on its end at the corner and drag. Only that wall's end moves.

*Update, 2026-11-08:* decided in [0130](../decisions/0130-2026-11-08-learning-the-editor.md). Pressing `Alt` mid-drag detaches the wall too, and it stays detached until the drag ends. Rather than a hint just for this, the editor gets one hint line that says what you can do at every step, and a **Keyboard and mouse** sheet (`?`) listing every move. Both ship in 1.0.0; a guided tour and a user guide come after.
