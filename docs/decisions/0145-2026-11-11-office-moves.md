# 0145 — Office moves: a move planner in 1.3, then future layouts, and no detection

- **Date:** 2026-11-11
- **Status:** Accepted
- **Refines:** [0104](0104-2026-10-21-v1-release-scope-and-support.md) and [0108](0108-2026-10-21-first-client-demo-feedback.md) (what comes after 1.0.0)

## Context
At a friend's demo on 2026-10-25, office moves came up as the main challenge: chairs and desks are rearranged, computers move and seats are reassigned ([the notes, point 1](../demos/feedback-2026-10-25.md#1-office-moves-are-a-lot-of-work)). Today seats change one at a time in the app, or through an import. The API's all-or-nothing batch endpoint for team moves and swaps ([0085](0085-2026-10-12-seat-assignment-api.md), part 7) has no screen yet. A device keeps its tag and history when it moves ([0022](0022-2026-10-08-device-asset-data.md)), but moving it is a manual edit. The notes also listed ways to detect where a PC is.

The owner went through it on 2026-11-11.

## Options considered
- Which part first:
  - **Seats, with devices following their person** ✅, then furniture.
  - Seats only: the smallest step, but devices would stay a manual edit in every move.
  - Detecting where PCs are: the biggest win if it works, but it depends on each client's network, and moves HSP toward network software.
  - Furniture first: seats change in every move, furniture less often, so it comes second.
- When the move planner ships:
  - 1.1, beside AD sync: soonest after 1.0.0 without holding it up, but it changes the order 0104 and 0108 set.
  - In 1.0.0: customers would have it from day one, but 1.0.0 would wait a few more weeks.
  - **1.3, after Kubernetes** ✅: keeps the order 0104 and 0108 set.
- Detecting where a PC is (switch port, the computer name an AD sign-in comes from, Wi-Fi, QR codes on desks):
  - Not now, and ask clients which source they'd allow: keeps the question open, while HSP stays away from network data meanwhile.
  - QR codes on desks, after the planner: needs no network access, but still has HSP find computers on its own rather than an admin placing them.
  - **Never** ✅: HSP stays away from network data, the line the first demo drew.

## Decision
1. **First step: a move planner, in 1.3.** Pick people or a team, place them on their new seats, see the plan before and after, and apply it all at once through the batch endpoint. *(The owner's choice)*
2. **Devices follow their person.** When a move changes someone's seat, HSP offers to move the devices issued to them along with it. *(The owner's choice)*
3. **Second step: furniture rearranging, with "future layouts".** A floor's next layout is prepared ahead of moving day. It comes after the planner. Its release, and how a future layout relates to drafts and published versions ([0015](0015-2026-10-08-floor-plan-versioning.md), [0081](0081-2026-10-10-drafts-and-changesets.md)), are decided when it's designed. *(The owner's choice; leaving the release open is Claude's)*
4. **HSP never detects where a PC is.** It reads nothing from the network, from sign-ins or from desk scans to find a computer. Moves stay something an admin does. This keeps the line the first demo drew between HSP and network software ([0108](0108-2026-10-21-first-client-demo-feedback.md), point 6). *(The owner's choice)*

## Consequences
- 1.1 is AD sync, 1.2 Kubernetes, and 1.3 the move planner. Bundle signing and an SBOM are still planned for 1.3 or 1.4 ([0104](0104-2026-10-21-v1-release-scope-and-support.md)).
- The planner's screens are designed in their own records before they're built.
- The other points of the 2026-10-25 notes (starting from existing plans, Material UI, the frontend's file layout) are still to be gone through.
